Overview

Skilled workers in Nigeria have no structured way to market their services, manage bookings, or get paid securely. They rely on informal referrals, which means inconsistent income and no real professional presence online.

A confusing or untrustworthy provider experience doesn't just frustrate workers. It drives them off the platform entirely, and takes their side of the marketplace with them.

Designing for a customer is one thing. What changes when you design for the person on the other side of that job?This section covers the specific challenge of building the provider side.

The Challenge

I was designing this provider side alongside the customer flow, at the same time, within a two-week hackathon. That meant serving two very different user types (someone looking for help, and someone offering it) with one coherent design system that still felt like a single product.

What did that shared system actually look like for the person doing the work?This section shows the provider dashboard, from wireframe to finished screens.

So what features actually made the provider side work?This section covers the core features built for service providers.

Key Features

  • Service showcase profile, with skills, ratings, and availability
  • Job request management: accept, decline, or schedule
  • Secure in-app payment for completed tasks
  • Customer rating system to build reputation over time

So what did building both sides of this marketplace at once actually teach me?A short reflection to close things out.

Learnings

Balancing two user groups in one marketplace taught me to think in systems, not just individual screens. Every feature built for the customer had a mirror on the provider side. Keeping both coherent under time pressure was the real design challenge.
